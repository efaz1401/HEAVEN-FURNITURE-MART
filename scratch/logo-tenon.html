Why it is this and not something else

It argues the site's own case

“The joint is the detail” appears on the homepage, the craft accordion and the about page. The overhanging tenon is that sentence as a shape: you can see how the piece holds together, which is what the copy says you are paying for.

Red stays an accent

tokens.css records a shipped full-bleed red overlay being reversed because red backed ten things and nine were small. The peg is one 3×11 bar — 5% of the mark's ink, an accent on a hairline frame rather than a field, consistent with that ruling.

It obeys the hard-edge law

Zero radius, square terminals, flat fills, no shadow and no gradient. Buttons are the only soft component on the site; a mark with rounded ends would read as borrowed from somewhere else.

It matches Jost 200

Stroke 3 in a 64 box is 4.7% — the stem weight of the display face beside it. A heavier mark is what the current placeholder favicon gets wrong: solid, tight and bold against a thin, wide, tracked wordmark.

It survives 16px

Four rectangles, all axis-aligned. No counters to fill in, no curve to alias, no detail that disappears. The counter between the stiles is 24 units against a 3-unit stroke, so the H never closes up, and the peg crosses the rail rather than sitting inside it — which is what keeps the red separable when it is down to one pixel.

It carries the AR reading too

A rail passing through a frame and emerging on the other side is also a piece entering a room. That is the site's other promise, and it comes free with the joinery reading rather than competing with it.
